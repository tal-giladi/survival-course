# Research Notes: How Professional Programs Structure Survival, Bushcraft, Wilderness First Aid and Preparedness Training

Compiled 2026-09-27. Purpose: to inform the design of a self-study course covering wilderness survival, bushcraft, wilderness first aid and emergency preparedness.

URL verification legend (checked 2026-09-27):

- [B] = the site blocks automated fetches (HTTP 403 or a CAPTCHA), but the URL showed up in current search-engine results or loaded through a second fetch tool. It will very likely open in a normal browser.

## Section A: How Programs Structure Their Curricula

### A.1 Program profiles

**1. NOLS Wilderness Medicine (WFA / WAFA / WFR / WEMT)**
- Tiers:
  - WFA: 16 h over 2 days.
  - WAFA: 40 h over 5 days.
  - WFR: about 80 h over 9-10 days, with two evening sessions.
  - WEMT: about 4 weeks, adding EMT-B.
  - Certifications last 2 years. Recertification can be done "down-tier": for example, a WFR can recertify through a WFR-recert, WAFA or WFA course. Minimum age is 16.
- Sequence:
  1. Patient assessment system (scene size-up, primary survey, secondary survey, SOAP notes).
  2. Trauma: spine, wounds, fractures and dislocations.
  3. Environmental: cold, heat, altitude, lightning, submersion, bites and stings.
  4. Medical: cardiac, respiratory, anaphylaxis, diabetes.
  5. Evacuation and long-term patient care decision-making.
- Pedagogy: "hands-on practice from the first day". Outdoor scenarios with simulated patients (moulage) run in all weather. Students work in small teams with leadership rotation. The practical share is high, roughly 50% or more of contact time on WFR.
- Sources: [NOLS WFR](https://www.nols.edu/en/coursefinder/courses/wilderness-first-responder-WFR/) [B]; [NOLS WAFA](https://www.nols.edu/en/coursefinder/courses/wilderness-advanced-first-aid-WAFA/) [B]; [NOLS WFA](https://www.nols.edu/en/coursefinder/courses/wilderness-first-aid-WFA/) [B].

**2. SOLO Schools (US, oldest wilderness medicine school, founded 1976)**
- Tiers:
  - WFA: 16 h over 2 days.
  - WFR: 72+ h over 8-10 days.
  - WEMT.
  - Certifications last 2 years.
- Sequence: both WFA and WFR use the same five blocks, at different depth: Response & Assessment → Musculoskeletal → Environmental Emergencies & Survival Skills → Soft Tissue → Medical Emergencies.
- Pedagogy: the WFR's extra time goes mostly into "much more hands-on practice", not new topics. Of the medical providers, SOLO is notable for folding survival skills into the medical syllabus.
- Sources: [SOLO WFA](https://www.soloschools.com/courses/wfa) [V]; [SOLO WFR](https://www.soloschools.com/courses/wfr) [V].

**3. American Red Cross Wilderness & Remote First Aid (WRFA)**
- Tiers: a single 2-day course (about 16 h). Prerequisites: current adult CPR/AED and age 14+.
- Sequence: first aid principles adapted for delayed EMS: assessment, then injuries, then environmental illness, then improvised care and evacuation decisions.
- Pedagogy: instructor-led, with skill checks and scenarios. It is designed as a lay-responder bridge from standard first aid, and it was developed with the Boy Scouts (BSA) and the Wilderness Medical Society.
- Source: [Red Cross WRFA](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/classes/wilderness-and-remote-first-aid/LP-00083300.html) [V].

**4. Boulder Outdoor Survival School (BOSS, Utah, founded 1968)**
- Tiers:
  - 7-day, 14-day and 28-day Field Expeditions.
  - Skills-based courses held at base camp: 7-Day Primitive Skills, and a 14-Day Primitive Living Intensive.
- Sequence on the field courses:
  1. An initial "impact" phase: minimal gear (knife, poncho, blanket), long walks on little food, and water finding.
  2. Survival priorities.
  3. Friction fire, starting with the bow drill; hand drill and fire plough are optional on the longer courses.
  4. Shelter.
  5. Then, on the 14-day: large-animal processing and rations, followed by foraging, trapping and fishing.
  6. A small-group or solo phase near the end.
- Pedagogy: immersion and deliberate hardship ("learning how to do more with less"), with self-knowledge under stress stated as a core learning outcome. The hands-on ratio is close to 100%. There is no certification.
- Sources: [BOSS Field Expedition](https://www.boss-inc.com/14-day-field-course/) [V]; [BOSS Primitive Skills](https://www.boss-inc.com/pages/program/primitive-skills) [V]; [BOSS FAQ](https://www.boss-inc.com/pages/faq) [V].

**5. Woodlore / Ray Mears (UK)**
- Tiers: 2-day introductions → Fundamental Bushcraft (7 days, Sunday evening to Saturday). The Fundamental course ends in a practical test that is "the gateway" to the Journeyman course, and further courses follow from there (tracking, canoe, etc.).
- Sequence: camp craft and tool safety → fire by several methods → shelter and bedding → water → cordage, containers and carving → plant and tree knowledge → cooking. Each skill is taught by demonstration, then practice, then application, and the pace rises across the week.
- Pedagogy: classic explain → demonstrate → practise → apply. It is cumulative (later skills rely on earlier ones), with an assessed gateway to the next level.
- Source: [Woodlore Fundamental Bushcraft](https://raymears.com/products/fundamental-bushcraft) [V].

**6. Institute for Outdoor Learning (IOL) Bushcraft Competency awards (UK, accredited)**
- Tiers:
  - Award: 5 units (edged tools, fire, water, tarp shelters & knots, campfire cooking). Requires a logbook of 60 quality hours of consolidated experience.
  - Certificate: 8 units (edged tools, fire, bow drill, water, debris shelters & bedding, tree ID & uses, natural navigation, cordage).
  - Diploma: 14 compulsory units plus 2 of 4 optional units. Requires the Certificate first.
  - Separately, there is a regulated Level 2 Bushcraft Skills Award.
- Pedagogy: training course, then consolidated logged experience, then independent assessment of competence. The assessment is aimed at leaders who run bushcraft for groups. This is the clearest published competency ladder in bushcraft and the best model for a self-study progression.
- Sources: [IOL Bushcraft hub](https://www.outdoor-learning.org/standards/iol-awards-and-accreditation/bushcraft.html) [V]; [Award](https://www.outdoor-learning.org/standards/iol-awards-and-accreditation/bushcraft/bushcraft-competency-award.html) [V]; [Certificate](https://www.outdoor-learning.org/standards/iol-awards-and-accreditation/bushcraft/bushcraft-competency-certificate.html) [V]; [Diploma](https://www.outdoor-learning.org/standards/iol-awards-and-accreditation/bushcraft/bushcraft-competency-diploma.html) [V].

**7. Karamat Wilderness Ways / Mors Kochanski (Alberta, Canada)**
- Tiers: week-long Warm-Weather and Cold-Weather Outdoor Living Skills courses. Karamat also sells the "e-Grand Syllabus", Kochanski's master skills list.
- Sequence: the Kochanski emphasis is shelter and sleeping warm first, because in the boreal forest the killer is cold. This leads to the "Super Shelter", fire lays for heat, the saw and axe, cordage and bark containers, and plant use.
- Pedagogy: mentor-led and field-based. Kochanski's framing was "the more you know, the less you carry", with survival treated as recreation practised in good conditions so the skills exist under bad ones.
- Sources: [Karamat](https://karamat.com/) [V]; [About Mors Kochanski](https://karamat.com/about-us/about-mors-kochanski/) [V].

**8. Tom Brown Jr.'s Tracker School (New Jersey)**
- Tiers: the Standard course (6 days) is the gateway to more than 100 advanced classes (Advanced Standard, tracking, scout, and philosophy streams). Some classes are online.
- Sequence of the Standard: shelter → water → fire → food (plants, trapping) → an introduction to tracking and awareness.
- Pedagogy: long lecture blocks (often late into the evening) plus practice. The narrative is built around Brown's teacher "Stalking Wolf".
- Caveat: Brown's biographical claims are disputed, and the philosophy stream is spiritual. Treat the school as a source for skills, not for evidence.
- Source: [Tracker School](https://www.trackerschool.com/en) [V].

**9. Wilderness Awareness School (Washington State): Kamana and tracking**
- Tiers: Kamana was a 4-level home-study naturalist program:
  1. Exploring Natural Mystery.
  2. The Path of the Naturalist.
  3. Deepening the Roots.
  4. The Complete Naturalist.
- Kamana was withdrawn from sale in January 2023. The school still runs in-person immersion programmes (Anake, tracking courses) and hosts CyberTracker evaluations.
- Pedagogy: Kamana is directly relevant to self-study. It was built on routines: the daily "sit spot", journaling, mapping, a "questions list", and naturalist research on local species (the "Awareness Trail" and "Resource Trail").
- Sources: [WAS](https://wildernessawareness.org/) [V]; [The Sunset of Kamana](https://wildernessawareness.org/articles/the-sunset-of-kamana/) [V].

**10. CyberTracker tracker evaluations (international)**
- Tiers: Track & Sign evaluations have a lower band (50 questions: 10 easy, 30 medium, 10 difficult) and an upper band (about 70 hard questions), scored as follows:
  - Level 1: 69-79%.
  - Level 2: 80-89%.
  - Level 3: 90-99%.
  - Professional: 100%.
  - Specialist evaluations are separate: 100% on very difficult spoor.
  - Trailing evaluations are a separate stream.
- Pedagogy: a pure assessment standard (not a course). Evaluations run in the field on real sign, with an evaluator who explains each answer. This is useful as a model for self-assessment with photo quizzes.
- Sources: [CyberTracker Tracker Certification 2018 (PDF)](https://www.cybertracker.org/downloads/tracking/CyberTracker-Tracker-Certification-2018.pdf) [V]; [Tracker Certification NA](https://trackercertification.com/track-and-sign-certifications/) [V].

**11. NASAR SARTECH (US ground SAR)**
- Tiers:
  - SARTECH III: entry level, written exam.
  - SARTECH II: field searcher. Preparation course is FUNSAR (Fundamentals of SAR), about 47 h over two weekends or 5 days. The exam is 145 written questions (pass 70%) plus a 6-station practical, which includes land navigation over a course of up to 2,000 m. WFA or higher has been required since 2021.
  - SARTECH I: crew leader.
- Sequence: SAR system and legal context → personal preparedness and the 24-hour pack → survival → navigation → search tactics (clue awareness, hasty/grid) → lost person behavior → tracking basics → rescue basics.
- Pedagogy: education is separate from certification (the exam is independent), and the practical stations are performance-based.
- Sources: [NASAR Ground SAR education](https://nasar.org/page/GSAR) [B]; [TNARS FUNSAR/SARTECH II](https://www.tnars.org/funsar-sartech-ii) [U, partner page from search].

**12. Mountain Training: Hill & Moorland Leader (UK)**
- Tiers: Lowland Leader → Hill & Moorland Leader → Mountain Leader (Summer, then Winter).
- HML route:
  - Register.
  - Log at least 20 Quality Hill Days before training.
  - 3-day training course (can be split 2+2 days).
  - Consolidation: log at least 40 QMDs in 3 or more UK/Irish upland areas.
  - Hold a 16-hour in-person first aid certificate.
  - 3-day assessment.
- Syllabus: navigation (the core), leadership & group management, hazards, weather, access/conservation/environment, the hill walking environment, equipment, emergency procedures, and campcraft (ML level).
- Pedagogy: the logged-experience model. Training teaches; consolidation builds the experience; assessment verifies.
- Sources: [HML qualification](https://www.mountain-training.org/qualifications/walking/hill-and-moorland-leader/) [V]; [Mountain Leader](https://www.mountain-training.org/qualifications/walking/mountain-leader/) [V].

**13. Outward Bound (US / UK / international)**
- Tiers: courses from 1 week to 3+ weeks, plus semester courses. There is no certification.
- Sequence: every course has three phases:
  1. Training phase: instructors teach core skills and build the crew culture.
  2. Main phase: students take on leadership roles and teach each other.
  3. Final phase: the crew runs the expedition, with instructors shadowing for safety.
- Solo: a period of solitary reflection (a few hours to 2 nights, depending on course length).
- Pedagogy: an experiential education cycle in which instructor control fades progressively. Character and leadership outcomes rank above technical skill.
- Sources: [What does a course look like](https://www.outwardbound.org/find-a-program/about-our-programs/what-does-a-course-look-like/) [U, from search]; [Outward Bound USA](https://www.outwardbound.org/) [V].

**14. NOLS Expeditions and Leadership**
- Curriculum pillars: leadership, outdoor skills, risk management, environmental studies. The leadership framework is 4 roles (designated leader, active follower, peer, self-leader), 7 skills (expedition behavior, competence, communication, judgment & decision-making, tolerance for adversity & uncertainty, self-awareness, vision & action) and 1 style.
- Sequence: instructors lead first. Students then take "leader of the day" roles, then small-group independent travel near the end.
- Sources: [NOLS core curriculum](https://www.nols.edu/expeditions/core-curriculum/) [B]; [NOLS Leadership](https://www.nols.edu/en/about/leadership/) [B].

**15. Wilderness Education Association (WEA)**
- Tiers: Outdoor Leadership Training Course (OLTC, a multi-week field course), then Certified Outdoor Leader (COL), then Certified Outdoor Educator (COE).
- Curriculum: the "18-point curriculum", which integrates technical skills with decision-making and judgment, leadership, teaching, environmental ethics, and risk management. It is often run through university outdoor-recreation degree programmes.
- Sources: [WEA](https://www.weainfo.org/) [V]; [OLTC](https://www.weainfo.org/oltc) [V].

**16. US military survival doctrine (public documents only)**
- FM 3-05.70 *Survival* (2002, formerly FM 21-76) was superseded by ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (Sept 2018). Both are approved for public release.
- FM 3-05.70 chapter order:
  1. Introduction.
  2. Psychology.
  3. Planning & kits.
  4. Basic survival medicine.
  5. Shelters.
  6. Water.
  7. Firecraft.
  8. Food.
  9. Survival use of plants.
  10. Poisonous plants.
  11. Dangerous animals.
  12. Field-expedient weapons, tools & equipment.
  13. Desert.
  14. Tropical.
  15. Cold weather.
  16. Sea.
  17. Expedient water crossings.
  18. Field-expedient direction finding.
  19. Signaling.
  20. Contact with people.
  21. Camouflage.
- FM 3-05.70 also contains the mnemonic "SURVIVAL" (Size up, Use all senses/Undue haste makes waste, Remember where you are, Vanquish fear & panic, Improvise, Value living, Act like the natives, Live by your wits).
- AFH 10-644 *SERE Operations* (Mar 2017, 650+ pp) is the USAF reference, organised by environment and activity.
- Public SERE principles: the "survival pattern" (shelter, fire, water, food, navigation, signaling) and the "will to survive". The resistance and escape content is out of scope and mostly not public.
- Sources: [ATP 3-50.21 record](https://armypubs.army.mil/ProductMaps/PubForm/Details.aspx?PUB_ID=1005316) [V]; [ATP 3-50.21 PDF](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/ARN12086_ATP%203-50x21%20FINAL%20WEB%202.pdf) [V]; [AFH 10-644 on Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/afh-10-644-survival-evasion-resistance-escape-operations-2017) [V]; [AF e-Publishing](https://www.e-publishing.af.mil/) [B].

## Section B: Synthesis

### B.1 The common core, present in almost every program
1. **A priority or assessment framework comes first.** Every medical course opens with the patient assessment system. Every survival course opens with "priorities of survival": a rule of 3s, or protection → location/signal → water → food (the military's Protection-Location-Acquisition order, and BOSS's priorities). Mountain Training opens with navigation as the thing that keeps a group out of trouble. The pattern is to teach the decision framework before the techniques.
2. **Thermoregulation outranks food.** Kochanski, Woodlore, IOL (fire and shelter units first), the military manuals and all the WFA syllabi treat hypothermia and hyperthermia and shelter/clothing as the top killers. Food comes late and gets the least time.
3. **Fire, water and shelter form the practical core of bushcraft.** The IOL Award, the Woodlore Fundamental, the Tracker Standard and the BOSS field courses all converge on edged-tool safety → fire → water → shelter → cordage/knots → cooking.
4. **Navigation is universal.** It is a core unit in NASAR, Mountain Training, the military manuals (direction finding) and IOL (natural navigation at Certificate level).
5. **Psychology and judgment.** It is explicit in the military manuals (SURVIVAL mnemonic, will to survive), BOSS (self-knowledge under stress), NOLS (judgment & decision-making; tolerance for adversity) and Outward Bound. Leach's and Gonzales's research gives the evidence base.
6. **Evacuation and signaling decisions.** WFR "go / no-go" evacuation logic, SAR "stay put / hug a tree", and military signaling doctrine.

### B.2 Sequencing patterns
- **Spiral curriculum (SOLO, NOLS WM):** the same five blocks at every tier, each time deeper. Well suited to self-study, because learners can do a light "WFA-depth" pass through all topics and then return for depth.
- **Ladder with gateways (IOL, Woodlore, CyberTracker, Mountain Training):** each tier has an assessment that unlocks the next. For self-study this becomes a practical checklist plus a photo/video self-evidence gate.
- **Train → consolidate → assess (Mountain Training, IOL):** the big insight is that consolidation, meaning logged real-world experience, is where competence is built. A self-study course should require a logbook (for example, "20 fires lit in wet conditions by 3 methods", "5 nights out in a self-built shelter").
- **Gradual release of responsibility (Outward Bound, NOLS Expeditions):** instructor-led → coached → independent → solo. In self-study this maps to guided drills in the backyard → short trips with a partner → solo overnight with a check-in plan.
- **Environment modules after the core (military manuals, AFH 10-644):** core skills first, then desert/tropical/cold/sea adaptations as add-ons.
- **Scenario-based integration (all medical courses; NASAR practicals):** skills are practised in isolation, then combined in timed scenarios. For self-study: written decision scenarios (branching), plus tabletop family-drill exercises for preparedness.

### B.3 Gaps across programs
- **Urban and disaster preparedness is mostly separate** from wilderness training (FEMA/Ready.gov, Red Cross, CERT). Few bushcraft or survival schools cover power outages, sheltering in place, evacuation logistics, documents or money, or communications plans.
- **Vehicle-based survival** (stranded in winter or desert) is thinly covered: NWS/Ready.gov car kits and military desert chapters, little else.
- **Long-duration self-reliance** (weeks to months) is covered only by BOSS 28-day and primitive-living courses. The evidence base is thin, and nutrition realities (protein poisoning, caloric deficit) are often romanticised.
- **Legal and jurisdictional content** (fires, foraging, trapping, fishing, wild camping) is rarely taught systematically, even though it varies strongly by country (see Section D).
- **Evidence grading:** bushcraft and survival schools rarely cite evidence. Medical programs track WMS guidelines. The field repeats myths (the "universal edibility test", sucking snakebite venom, "drink your urine", rubbing frostbite, treating 1-10-1 time windows as guarantees). A self-study course should flag the evidence level.
- **Psychology is under-taught** compared with its causal role in deaths (Leach, Gonzales, Koester's ISRID behavior data).
- **Accessibility and diversity of contexts:** most curricula are North American boreal/temperate or UK woodland. Desert, Mediterranean (relevant to Israel), and tropical content is mostly limited to the military manuals.

### B.4 What a self-study course can and cannot replace

| A self-study course CAN | A self-study course CANNOT |
|---|---|
| Teach frameworks, priorities and decision logic (assessment systems, survival priorities, evacuation criteria, rule of 3s with caveats) | Grant WFA/WFR or any recognised certification. These require in-person, hands-on assessment (Mountain Training even rejects online first aid for HML) |
| Deliver knowledge content: physiology, water treatment chemistry, weather, map symbols, legal rules | Supply real-time feedback on psychomotor skills (bow drill form, knife grip safety, splinting, a CPR depth/rate check) |
| Structure deliberate practice with logbooks and self-evidence (photos/video), mimicking IOL and Mountain Training consolidation | Safely supervise hazardous practice (axes, fire in dry conditions, cold exposure, water crossings, foraging ID) |
| Run branching scenario drills and tabletop preparedness exercises | Validate plant or fungus identification. Anything eaten must be checked by a qualified local expert and regional field guides |
| Build home and family preparedness (kits, plans, water storage) end to end, since it is mostly planning | Replicate the stress-inoculation value of immersion courses (BOSS, Outward Bound Solo) |
| Prepare learners to get more out of in-person courses, and to pass the written parts of NASAR or medical courses | Replace SAR or rescue team membership, avalanche courses (AIARE/SAIS), or swiftwater rescue |

Recommendation: position the course as "knowledge + structured practice + pointers to in-person certification". Put an explicit "go take a WFA" milestone and an "in-person bushcraft/navigation course" milestone into the progression.

### C.4 Water

### C.8 Physiology
- **"1-10-1" cold water immersion principle**, Gordon Giesbrecht (Univ. of Manitoba), Cold Water Boot Camp. [coldwaterbootcamp.com](https://www.coldwaterbootcamp.com/pages/1_10_60v2.html) [V]. Derived from immersion research: 1 min cold shock, 10 min meaningful movement, about 1 h before hypothermic unconsciousness.
  - Counterpoint: **"The 1-10-1 Myth"**, National Center for Cold Water Safety. [coldwatersafety.org](https://www.coldwatersafety.org/1-10-1-myth) [V]. Argues that these time windows are over-optimistic for many people; teach them as illustrative, not guaranteed.
- **TB MED 508 (cold)**, USARIEM [see C.5] [V]. **TB MED 507 (heat stress control and heat casualty management, 2022)**, via HPRC. [hprc-online.org](https://www.hprc-online.org/resources-partners/whec/educational-tools/tb-med-507-heat) [V]. **USARIEM Thermal & Mountain Medicine Division**. [usariem.health.mil](https://usariem.health.mil/index.cfm/research/divisions/tmmd) [V]. US Army environmental physiology research: the basis of work/rest tables and cold/heat guidance. (TB MED 505 covers altitude.)
- **ACSM Expert Consensus Statement on Exertional Heat Illness (2023)**, Roberts et al., *Curr Sports Med Rep* 22(4):134-149. [PubMed 37036463](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37036463/) [B]; [journal](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-csmr/fulltext/2023/04000/acsm_expert_consensus_statement_on_exertional_heat.10.aspx) [B]. The sports medicine consensus on recognition and "cool first, transport second".
- **IOM DRI for Water (2005)**. [See C.4] [V].
- **Wind Chill: NWS Wind Chill Chart and formula (2001)**. [chart](https://www.weather.gov/safety/cold-wind-chill-chart) [V]; [formula PDF](https://www.weather.gov/media/epz/wxcalc/windChill.pdf) [V]. The 2001 US/Canada index (T_wc = 35.74 + 0.6215T − 35.75V^0.16 + 0.4275TV^0.16, °F/mph). It replaced Siple & Passel (1945), which was based on the freezing of water in a cylinder in Antarctica and overstated chill. Siple, P.A. & Passel, C.F. (1945), *Proc. Am. Philos. Soc.* 89:177-199 [U].
- **NIOSH Heat Stress**, CDC. [cdc.gov/niosh](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/heat-stress/about/index.html) [B]. Occupational heat criteria.

### C.9 First aid and wilderness medicine
- **Wilderness Medical Society (WMS)** and its **Clinical Practice Guidelines** (published in *Wilderness & Environmental Medicine*, which is published by SAGE from 2024).
