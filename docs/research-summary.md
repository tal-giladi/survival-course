# Research summary

Full notes, program profiles and every checked URL: [research-notes.md](research-notes.md)
(≈150 URLs checked on 2026-09-27; each tagged verified / blocked-but-confirmed / unverified).

## What was compared

16 programs across six families:

| Family | Programs studied | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Wilderness medicine | NOLS (WFA 16 h → WAFA 40 h → WFR ~80 h), SOLO, American Red Cross WRFA, WMS guidelines | Patient-assessment system first; spiral curriculum; ~50 % hands-on scenarios; practical + written exam |
| Survival / primitive | BOSS (7/14/28-day), Tom Brown Tracker School | Immersion; priorities → fire → shelter → food → solo |
| Bushcraft | Woodlore (Ray Mears), IOL Bushcraft Award/Certificate/Diploma, Karamat (Kochanski) | Ladder with gateway tests; tools → fire → shelter → water → crafts; logged practice |
| Tracking / naturalist | CyberTracker evaluations, Wilderness Awareness School (Kamana) | Scored field evaluations; routines-based self-study |
| SAR & leadership | NASAR SARTECH, Mountain Training Hill & Moorland Leader, NOLS expeditions, Outward Bound, WEA | Train → consolidate (logged days) → assess; gradual release of responsibility |
| Doctrine / government | US Army ATP 3-50.21, USAF AFH 10-644, FEMA Ready.gov, CDC/EPA water, NWS | Psychology → planning/kits → medicine → shelter → water → fire → food → environments → navigation → signaling |

## Findings that shaped this course

1. **Framework before technique.** Every medical course starts with the patient-assessment
   system; every survival program starts with priorities. → Stage 1 is the decision system
   (loop + 12 questions), and every later lesson reuses it.
2. **Thermoregulation outranks food.** All programs treat hypothermia/hyperthermia, clothing and
   shelter as the top killers; food comes late. → Stage 1 includes a heat-budget lesson and
   simulator; food is Stage 6; the recommended path pulls physiology forward.
3. **Fire, water, shelter form the practical core** and appear in the same order in bushcraft
   ladders. → Stages 3–5, with Stage 1 providing the "first-hour" versions.
4. **Competence comes from consolidation, not courses.** Mountain Training and IOL require
   logged real-world practice between training and assessment. → Physical skills can only be
   self-marked *Practiced / Competent*, never auto-awarded; exercises act as a logbook.
5. **Spiral + scenario integration.** SOLO/NOLS revisit the same blocks at increasing depth and
   integrate through timed scenarios. → Stage 1 gives light versions of fire/water/shelter/
   signaling; later stages deepen them; capstones integrate; spaced review re-surfaces them.
6. **Environment modules after the core** (military manuals). → Environments rotate through
   examples; environment-specific depth arrives in Stages 3–5, 8, 17 and the capstones.

## Gaps in existing programs this course fills

- **Urban/disaster, vehicle and long-duration** are thin or separate in most programs → Stages 16–18.
- **Law and jurisdiction** are rarely taught systematically → "Law varies" callouts and a
  portal list (US, UK, Canada, Australia/NZ, Nordics, Israel).
- **Evidence grading.** Bushcraft literature repeats myths (universal edibility test, sucking
  venom, rubbing frostbite, 1-10-1 as a guarantee, Siple–Passel wind chill). → The course
  prefers WMS/CDC/NWS/USARIEM sources and flags myths explicitly in "Common mistakes".
- **Psychology is under-taught** relative to its causal role (Leach, Gonzales, Koester's ISRID)
  → psychology appears in Stage 1 and gets its own Stage 15.
- **Non-temperate climates** (desert, Mediterranean, tropical) are under-covered outside military
  manuals → examples and capstones rotate through them.

## What a self-study course can and cannot do

| Can | Cannot |
|---|---|
| Teach frameworks, priorities, decision logic | Grant WFA/WFR or any certification |
| Teach the science (physiology, water chemistry, weather) | Give real-time feedback on psychomotor skills |
| Structure deliberate practice with logs | Safely supervise hazardous practice (axes, fire in dry conditions, cold exposure, water crossings) |
| Run branching scenario drills | Validate plant or fungus identification |
| Build home/family preparedness end to end | Replicate immersion stress inoculation |

**Positioning:** knowledge + structured practice + explicit milestones pointing to in-person
training — a hands-on WFA/WAFA course (after Stage 9), a navigation/orienteering course (after
Stage 2), and a bushcraft course (after Stage 7).

## Source hierarchy used in the course

1. Clinical practice guidelines and consensus statements (WMS, ACSM, ICAR MedCom).
2. Government agencies (CDC, EPA, WHO, NWS, FEMA, USARIEM, land agencies).
3. Professional training bodies (NOLS, SOLO, Red Cross, Mountain Training, IOL, NASAR).
4. Standard textbooks (Freedom of the Hills, NOLS Wilderness Medicine, Auerbach, Kochanski).
5. Practitioner books and popular works — used for breadth, with caveats stated.
