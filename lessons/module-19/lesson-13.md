# 19.13 · Final assessment

Unfamiliar emergencies are solved by transferable reasoning. The assessment deliberately mixes domains because real situations do.

## Explanation

This assessment tests whether you can **reason** like a survivor, not whether you remember facts. Most questions are short scenarios where several answers sound plausible — the best answer is the one that removes the biggest risk for the least cost, with a bias toward reversible actions.

Your score is broken down by domain. Anything under 80 % points to the stages to revisit; missed questions join your spaced-review queue.

[Simulation: Final assessment](../../simulations/final-assessment/index.html)

Judgment and prioritization across every domain of the course.

> [!WARNING]
> **What a pass means**
>
> A high score means your *decision making* is sound. It does not make you physically competent. Your next steps are on the Skills page: practise every physical skill until you can mark it Practiced, and take a hands-on wilderness first aid course, a navigation course and a bushcraft course.

## Examples

Questions span forest, desert, subarctic, tropical, mountain, coastal and urban settings, and every stage of the course.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing the most dramatic action instead of the highest-value one.
- Ignoring reversibility and daylight.
- Treating a good score as proof of physical competence.

## Practical exercises

### Take the final assessment

> [!CAUTION]
> **Virtual only.** Simulate only. Do not attempt physically.

Level 4 (Integrated) · 🖥️ Virtual only · about 60 min

**Steps**

1. Complete the assessment without references.
2. Write down every domain under 80 %.
3. Schedule review of those stages and redo their capstones.

**You have it when**

- Overall ≥ 80 % and no domain below 60 %.

Builds the skill: First-hour survival plan.

### Build your training plan

Level 3 (Safe physical) · 🏠 Home · about 45 min

**Steps**

1. Open the Skills page and list every physical skill not yet Practiced.
2. For each, choose where and with whom you will practise it, within its safety class.
3. Book a hands-on wilderness first aid course (WFA or WAFA) and a navigation course.

**You have it when**

- A dated plan for every physical skill.
- A first aid course booked.

Builds the skill: Run the Observe–Assess–Prioritize–Plan–Act–Reassess loop.

## Scenario question

You have finished the course and score 85 % overall, but 55 % in first aid.

**What is your best next step?**

1. Plan a remote solo trip to test yourself in real conditions.
2. Revisit Stage 9, redo capstone 6 and book a hands-on WFA/WAFA course.
3. Retake the assessment until you pass first aid by memorising answers.
4. Ignore it; first aid is unlikely to be needed on most trips.

<details>
<summary>Best choice and debrief</summary>

**Best: 2.** The assessment exists to find gaps. Close them with review, scenarios and — for first aid especially — hands-on training.

- **1.** Your weakest domain is the one you would need most if something went wrong alone.
- **2.** Best: targets the gap with both review and real training before remote trips.
- **3.** Memorising answers doesn’t build judgment or hands-on skill.
- **4.** Injury is among the most common wilderness emergencies.

</details>

## Summary

- Judgment and prioritization transfer; memorised tricks do not.
- Use the domain breakdown to target review.
- Knowledge + logged practice + hands-on courses = capability.
